## Solar: applying wind-grade rigour to utility-scale PV

Utility-scale solar has matured into a discipline that rewards the same analytical depth Aldott has long applied to wind — structural design under load, energy yield prediction, and grid compliance engineering. Our direction in solar is to bring that depth to PV projects, not to compete on installation volume.

### What this means in practice

- **Structural and energy yield analysis.** Applying our loads-analysis heritage to PV structures — foundation and racking loads, wind and snow load cases on module arrays, and single-axis tracker design validated against site-specific structural criteria.
- **Irradiance and yield modelling.** Site irradiance modelling combined with P50/P90 energy yield analysis, giving developers and financiers the same statistically grounded confidence in solar output that AEP modelling has long provided in wind.
- **Module and system-level engineering discipline.** Design and technical due diligence referenced against IEC 61215 and IEC 61730 module qualification standards, bifacial PV performance characterisation, and DC/AC ratio optimisation to manage inverter loading and reduce levelised cost of energy (LCOE).
- **Grid integration engineering.** Grid code compliance analysis and interconnection studies that treat solar generation as part of a wider grid-integrated asset, not a standalone installation.
- **Wind-solar hybrid convergence.** Co-located and hybrid wind-solar projects are becoming a standard configuration for developers seeking to maximise land use and grid connection value. Our direction is to bring integrated engineering — shared yield modelling, shared grid studies, shared structural governance — to these hybrid assets, rather than treating wind and solar as separate engineering exercises run in parallel.

Solar, in Aldott's future practice, is not a new business line bolted onto wind. It is the same engineering method — structural rigour, yield certainty, grid discipline — applied to a second generation technology.

## Hydrogen: engineering the link between generation and infrastructure

Green hydrogen is, in engineering terms, a downstream application of renewable generation — and that is precisely how Aldott intends to approach it. Our direction in hydrogen builds directly from work already underway in our portfolio, including technical and commercial coordination on the HT1 Aberdeen Bay project, where offshore wind generation is being integrated with hydrogen production. That project is an early indicator of the direction we intend to formalise and scale.

### What this means in practice

- **Electrolysis and stack integration.** Engineering support for PEM and alkaline electrolyser stack integration, sizing electrolysis capacity against the generation profile of the connected wind or solar asset rather than against a generic load assumption.
- **Power-to-X engineering.** Technical planning for Power-to-X (P2X) pathways, where hydrogen output is engineered as an input to downstream products — ammonia, synthetic fuels, or industrial feedstock — rather than an end product in isolation.
- **Storage, compression, and transport.** Engineering input on hydrogen compression, storage system design, and transport logistics, addressing the practical infrastructure gap between generation and offtake.
- **Offshore wind-to-hydrogen integration.** Direct extension of our offshore wind engineering base — turbine-to-electrolyser interface design, offshore versus onshore electrolysis trade-off analysis, and the technical coordination work already demonstrated on the Aberdeen Bay type of project.
- **Safety, certification, and economics.** Design and documentation referenced against IEC 62282 fuel cell technology standards and ATEX/DSEAR requirements for hazardous-area equipment, alongside levelised cost of hydrogen (LCOH) analysis to support offtake agreement negotiations and investment decisions.
- **Bridging generation and infrastructure.** Hydrogen projects fail as often on integration engineering as they do on chemistry or economics — the gap between a wind or solar asset and a certified, bankable hydrogen production and offtake system. That is the gap Aldott's engineering methodology is built to close.

## How we get there

This is a direction, not a switch. We are approaching solar and hydrogen the way we approach every engineering problem: incrementally, with evidence at each stage.

### Phase 1 — Extend the methodology
Apply our existing R&D and certification-grade engineering methodology — loads analysis, yield modelling, technical documentation discipline — to solar PV engineering problems, using wind as the reference discipline.

### Phase 2 — Build hybrid delivery capability
Develop project delivery capability for hybrid and co-located assets: wind-solar hybrid generation projects and wind-to-hydrogen integration projects, extending directly from work already underway on projects such as HT1 Aberdeen Bay.

### Phase 3 — Full-spectrum clean energy R&D
Establish Aldott as a full-spectrum clean energy engineering house — one R&D methodology, applied consistently across wind, solar, and hydrogen, for developers and OEMs who need generation and infrastructure engineered as a single system rather than as separate contracts.
